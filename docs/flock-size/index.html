The lay rates behind the breed presets are year-round realistic averages for healthy backyard hens in their first two laying years — not hatchery-brochure peaks:

PresetAverageWinter (~60%)
Production breeds5.5 /wk3.3 /wk
Solid layers4.5 /wk2.7 /wk
Heritage / dual-purpose3.5 /wk2.1 /wk

With "size for winter" on, the flock is sized so the winter rate still covers your need — extension guidance notes production falls substantially as daylight shortens (without supplemental lighting); we use ~60% of average as a middle-of-the-road planning figure. Real flocks range wider: first-winter pullets often lay right through, while heritage breeds in a far-north January can stop almost entirely. The flip side is a summer surplus, which is how everyone accidentally becomes an egg dealer.

Not modeled: the first months before pullets start laying, molting pauses (often 8–12 weeks, sometimes longer, usually in fall), and the gentle decline after year two — all reasons the standing advice is round up. For breed selection and flock care, your local cooperative extension service is the gold standard.
