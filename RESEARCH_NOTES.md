# RESEARCH NOTES — Session 0 (2026-07-26)

## Question: where can a solo, zero-budget tool portfolio actually win?

### Finding 1 — Generic calculators are a locked market
Similarweb/Semrush data (checked July 2026): the generic calculator space is
dominated by omnicalculator.com, calculator.net, calculatorsoup.com,
rapidtables.com, inchcalculator.com, gigacalculator.com. Omni Calculator draws
~76% of traffic from organic search and sits near the top of its category
rankings. These are old, high-authority domains. A new site cannot win generic
head terms ("concrete calculator", "bmi calculator") on any reasonable timeline.
**Implication:** do not build generic; do not depend on SEO.

### Finding 2 — Non-tech niches remain underserved
Multiple 2026 sources on micro-SaaS gaps converge on the same reason: founders
build for industries they know (dev tools, marketing, PM), so skilled trades,
local services, and hobbyist/lifestyle niches stay underserved despite
willingness to engage/pay — their members aren't on Product Hunt or Hacker News.
**Implication:** pick an audience tech people don't build for, and meet them
where they already gather.

### Finding 3 — Distribution beats product in this category
Free tools spread when shared inside communities (subreddits, Facebook groups,
forums, newsletters). A tool that one moderator or popular member likes can
outperform months of SEO. Shareability requirements: instantly useful, mobile
friendly, transparent about its math, no signup wall, no ads at launch.

## Niche selection

Criteria: community size/activity × calculation density of the hobby ×
underservedness × evergreen-ness × later monetization adjacency.

**Chosen: homesteading / backyard self-sufficiency.**
- Multi-million-member communities (r/homestead, r/BackYardChickens, large FB
  groups), highly active, non-tech, sharing-friendly.
- The hobby is full of recurring sizing/planning/economics questions.
- Existing tools are scattered blog widgets with poor UX.
- Evergreen and growing; monetization adjacency (equipment affiliates, planners).

**Backup: RV/vanlife power systems** (solar sizing, battery math, weight
distribution). Similar dynamics; slightly more existing tooling.

**To validate in Session 1** (this was desk research; ground-truth it):
browse the communities, list the 10 most frequent calculable questions, confirm
the first tool answers a question people actually keep asking.

## Risks logged honestly
- Community self-promotion rules can be strict → transparent maker-posts only,
  read each community's rules first, favor communities that welcome free tools.
- "Passive" failure mode: tools ship but nobody sees them → distribution is a
  first-class task every session, not an afterthought.
- Niche could be shallower than it looks → backup niche is pre-selected and the
  architecture (static tool pages) transfers 1:1.
