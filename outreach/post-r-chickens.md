**Title:**
Built a free calculator that answers "how many hens do we actually need?" (and it's honest about winter)

**Body:**

The question that starts every flock: "we eat about a dozen and a half a week,
how many hens is that?" And the answer everyone learns the hard way: enough
hens for July is not enough hens for January.

So I built a small free calculator that does the whole thing properly:

https://jbourgogne.github.io/homestead-toolshed/flock-size/

You put in your household's weekly egg habit (plus any dozens you want to
share or sell), pick a breed preset — production breeds, solid layers, or
heritage/dual-purpose — and it sizes your flock. There's a "size for winter"
toggle that plans around the cold-months lay rate instead of the yearly
average. The tradeoff runs both ways and the tool says so: winter-sized
flocks drown you in summer eggs, average-sized flocks run short in January.

No ads, no signup, no tracking — it all runs in your browser, and the math is
shown on the page. It's part of a free-tools experiment I'm running, with an
AI assistant doing a lot of the building. There's also a coop & run size
calculator and a "what do your eggs really cost" one on the same site.

If your flock's real-world numbers disagree with what it told you, tell me
and I'll fix it.
