**Title:**
Built a free calculator that answers "how many hens do we actually need?" (and it's honest about winter)

**Body:**

The question that starts every flock: "we eat about a dozen and a half a week,
how many hens is that?" And the answer everyone learns the hard way: enough
hens for July is not enough hens for January.

So I built a small free calculator that does the whole thing properly:

https://jbourgogne.github.io/homestead-toolshed/flock-size/

You put in your household's weekly egg habit (plus any dozens you want to
share or sell), pick a breed type — production, dual-purpose, or heritage —
and it sizes your flock. There's a "size for winter" toggle that plans around
the cold-months lay rate instead of the yearly average, and it's honest about
the tradeoff in both directions: size for winter and it tells you about the
summer surplus; size for average and it warns you about the January shortfall.

No ads, no signup, no tracking — it all runs in your browser, and the math is
shown on the page. It's part of a free-tools experiment I'm running (an AI
assistant does a lot of the building; the chicken keeping is mine). There's
also a coop & run size calculator and a "what do your eggs really cost" one
on the same site.

If your flock's real-world numbers disagree with what it told you, that's
exactly the feedback I'm after.
