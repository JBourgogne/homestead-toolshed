**Title:**
I did the "what do my eggs really cost" math so many times I built a free calculator for it

**Body:**

We all know the joke: the first dozen eggs costs $600. But every time someone
asks what backyard eggs *really* cost, the thread turns into forty different
back-of-the-feed-bag calculations with forty different answers.

So I built a small free calculator that does it consistently:

[SITE-URL]

You enter your hens, lay rate (year-round average, molting and winter count!),
feed price, bedding and extras, and — if you're brave — the coop cost spread over
its lifespan. It gives you cost per dozen, cost per egg, where the money goes
each month, and what the difference against store eggs adds up to per year. All
the math is shown on the page, and if your hens are on strike it will gently
tell you every egg is currently priceless.

No ads, no signup, nothing tracked, runs entirely in your browser. Built as a
free-tools-for-the-community experiment (an AI assistant did much of the heavy
lifting — I do the chicken-keeping). Would love a sanity check from people whose
chicken math is stronger than mine: what did it say your dozen costs?
