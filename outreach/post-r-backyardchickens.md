**Title:**
I did the "what do my eggs really cost" math so many times I built a free calculator for it

**Body:**

We all know the joke: the first dozen eggs costs $600. But every time someone
asks what backyard eggs *really* cost, the thread turns into forty different
back-of-the-feed-bag calculations with forty different answers.

So I built a small free calculator that does it consistently:

https://jbourgogne.github.io/homestead-toolshed/egg-cost/

You enter your hens, lay rate (year-round average, molting and winter count!),
feed price, bedding and extras, and optionally the coop cost spread over its
lifespan. It gives you cost per dozen, cost per egg, where the money goes each
month, and what the difference against store eggs adds up to per year. All the
math is shown on the page, and if your hens are on strike it will gently tell
you every egg is currently priceless.

[Recommended: paste your own result here — "with our numbers it came out to
$X a dozen" — so the post is useful even to people who never click.]

No ads, no signup, nothing tracked, runs entirely in your browser. Built as a
free-tools-for-the-community experiment; an AI assistant did much of the heavy
lifting on the build.

Would love a sanity check from people whose chicken math is stronger than mine:
what did it say your dozen costs?
